# The observer in the world

Daniel John Murray · The Murray Research Programme  
Public direction · 14 September 2026

**How does a finite, changing part of the world acquire a usable perspective on that same world, and use it to change both itself and its surroundings?**

The programme begins with boundedness and develops through observation, predictive state, temporal operations, resources and viable action. Its ambition is a connected scientific account of observer and environment. Its progress is measured by supported predictions, discriminating experiments and useful interventions.

## Current research focus

**Develop the functioning local observer model into a biologically grounded information-and-action loop.**

The intended sequence is: observe; predict; choose a feasible measurement or action; compare its outcome; identify a missing distinction; propose a revised state; validate the revision; retain or reject it.

The current local prototype now performs noisy sensing, joint inference over mechanism and calibration, prediction and costed terminal action. Its next bridge asks: **can a feasible measurement reveal a distinction that changes a useful action while that action is still available?** Identifiability, future prediction and available decision headroom are separate tests. Before searching for a better controller, calculate whether the permitted actions leave enough possible improvement to meet the declared target. The initial work uses declared enzyme models, followed by a separate biological-data and calibration gate. Recovering a mechanism supplied to a simulation is a test of the instrument, not discovery of that mechanism in nature.

One observation and a history of observations have different acquisition costs. An intervention must be compared with strong controls at the same relevant budgets. Baselines, meaningful improvement thresholds and failure conditions precede evaluation. Private experiment details and unpublished proofs are outside this public direction record.

## Let evidence change the map

A distinction earns a place when losing it changes a supported prediction or available action beyond the permitted error. A larger representation must also be compared with a simpler one. New tasks may make previously discarded distinctions relevant, so original evidence and the reason for compression remain traceable.

A supported revision updates its actual dependants. Changed assumptions can suspend an application while leaving an independent theorem intact. Failed candidates and stronger conventional explanations remain part of the record. No model may improve its apparent result merely by relaxing its own acceptance threshold.

There are two kinds of change: the physical system evolves during an experiment, and scientific knowledge changes when evidence warrants a revision. A moving simulation is not itself a new discovery.

## What would count as progress?

- A new measured condition is predicted before its outcome is examined.
- A feasible experiment separates explanations that previously made the same predictions.
- An additional state variable or history record improves a held-out prediction or action enough to justify its cost.
- A simpler description preserves the required outcomes with a justified error bound.
- A scoped mathematical result survives proof scrutiny, with its physical applications tested separately.

Formal implication, numerical correctness, empirical support, causal evidence and scientific novelty are separate questions. The long-term interest includes perception, self-representation and subjective experience; no present diagram or simulation establishes a theory of consciousness or a complete theory of physics.
